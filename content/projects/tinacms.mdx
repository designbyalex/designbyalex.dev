intro: |-
  I led the UI/UX redesign of the TinaCMS admin for v4, end to end: competitor research, a coded prototype, and a Storybook design system that the new admin is built from.

  TinaCMS has let teams edit their sites visually since 2019, with content kept in Git. Its admin grew one feature at a time for six years, and while the editing worked, the experience had fallen behind its rivals on feature parity, UX and cross-platform usability. v4 is a full rewrite of TinaCMS, and <mark>the moment to redesign the admin from the ground up.</mark>

  I worked as a design engineer, directing AI agents through every stage while making every design decision myself. In six weeks that covered 11 surfaces of the admin, each one designed in code, reviewed and handed to the TinaCMS developer to build.

In the code, blue outnumbered Tina orange nearly seven to one.\

    subheading: Always knowing where you stand
    headingSize: statement
    blocks:
      - _template: body
        body: |-
          In the current admin, editors couldn’t always tell where they stood. Unsaved changes looked different from view to view, the active branch dropped off some screens, and saving or switching branch could fail without a clear reason.\
          \
          I designed a save model editors can read at a glance. The Save button shows saving, saved and failed states, with Retry when something goes wrong. The active branch shows in every view, and editors can switch it in place, save to a new branch, or discard and resume their unsaved work.

    heading: Errors
    subheading: From a blocked Save to a clear way forward
    headingSize: statement
    blocks:
      - _template: body
        body: |-
          A failed save is one of the problems none of the 13 platforms had solved well. I grounded the new pattern in primary research from GOV.UK, the NHS and the Government of Canada design systems.\
          \
          Errors appear only after a Save attempt, never while someone is still typing. A blocked Save becomes “Resolve errors”, which opens a summary pane listing every problem, and count chips on collapsed groups show where the hidden ones are.

        body: |-
          Before deleting a file, an editor needs to know where it’s used. The current admin hid that, and none of the 13 rivals showed it either.\

          I redesigned the library and its states, an upload card with progress for each file, delete that shows where a file is used, a picker that keeps the form and preview in view, and the edge cases: permissions, protected branches and missing images. All of it works at desktop, tablet and phone widths, and I checked it against 60+ accessibility scenarios.
